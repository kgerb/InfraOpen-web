Find water, a toilet, and a place to park for the night.

InfraOpen is a free map from and for the community: for people outdoors, anyone who is thirsty, anyone who needs a toilet quickly, and everyone who sleeps in their car. It brings open public data from across Germany into one clear app and shows where every fact comes from.

Who it's for

For everyone who is out and about

Outdoor people

Hikers, cyclists, paddlers and climbers who want to know where the next water point or toilet is before they set off.

The thirsty

Refill your bottle at a public fountain instead of buying water in plastic. The map shows which ones people found working recently.

The urgent

Need a toilet, quickly? See the nearest public and partner toilets, nearest first, with accessibility where the source knows it.

Car sleepers

Find parking and designated overnight sites, with slope, morning sun and nearby water and toilets worked out in advance.

On the map

What InfraOpen shows today

Water, toilets and parking cover all of Germany, imported from OpenStreetMap for every federal state and from city open data where it exists.

Drinking water

Public fountains and refill points, with the source of each entry and the latest community check.

WC

Toilets

Public toilets and partner toilets in shops and cafés, listed nearest first and available offline.

P

Parking

Car parks and parking areas, with surface, fee and access as far as the data tells.

Overnight spots Pilot: Baden-Württemberg + Bayern

Designated sites keep their official status and fee. Other spots show badges such as Designated, Free or Forest. A badge never means overnight stays are allowed.

Sun and shade

Shadows from terrain and trees for any hour 12 hours back or ahead, with sunshine from the German Weather Service.

Terrain and aerial imagery

Hillshade from official elevation models, and aerial photos from the federal states' open imagery.

Community reports

Tap Usable, or report a problem. Add a missing fountain, toilet, car park or overnight site straight from the map.

Photos and profiles

Photos of places from open sources, each reviewed before it appears. Profiles are public by default, and you can switch yours off.

How it works

Public data, shown honestly

Plenty of useful data about public infrastructure already exists, spread across city portals, state survey offices and OpenStreetMap. InfraOpen collects it, keeps track of where it came from, and lets the people on the ground say whether it's still true.

Every fact shows its source

Each place lists where its data comes from and when it was last confirmed. Uncertain data is shown as uncertain.

source: OpenStreetMapchecked: 3 days ago

The community keeps it fresh

A quick "Usable" or "Problem" after a visit is often more current than any official list.

Works without signal

The app keeps nearby places on your phone, so you can still find water in a valley with no reception. Reports upload once you're back online.

Non-profit, no ads

InfraOpen is a community project. It doesn't sell data, show ads or track you.

Careful with your location

Weather and sunshine are looked up by district. Your exact position is never sent for them.

Reviewed before it's public

Photos from open sources go through a review queue first. Problem reports are anonymous, and you can hide your profile at any time.

Where it stands

Early days

InfraOpen started in Freiburg and now covers Germany. It's tested by a small group before anyone else can install it.

In the beta now

  • Water, toilets and parking for all of Germany
  • Overnight spots in Baden-Württemberg and Bayern
  • Sun, shade, terrain and aerial maps
  • Reports, photos and public profiles

Being worked on

  • More photos of places, from open street-level imagery
  • Overnight spots for more federal states
  • Polishing the app with beta testers

Not yet

  • A public download
  • An iPhone app
  • Public source code
